# The Alp Discipline

## Purpose

This is a decision discipline. It is not a project management method, not a craft guide, and
not a compliance standard. It has a single job: **to help a human or an AI agent decide better
at the moment they meet a boundary.** How to apply it is left to the reader; it produces no
bureaucracy of its own. **Regardless of field, sector, or role**, a human and an AI agent alike
should be able to read the same four principles and work out what to do in their own context.

## Seven load-bearing definitions

- **Boundary.** A line it has been decided not to cross. It may be soft (it may be departed
  from with justification), hard (only a necessity opens it), or foundational (it is changed
  only through a dedicated process).
- **Owner.** The party who *today* holds the authority to interpret and change a boundary. The
  owner is not necessarily whoever first drew the line: **origin ≠ authority.** It varies by
  context: a manager, a client, an ethics board, a funder, or, in solo work, *you at another
  moment* (see Principle 2).
- **Goal ≠ approach.** The goal is *what* you are trying to achieve; the approach is *how* you
  are currently trying to achieve it. Separating the two is the single most useful move in
  this discipline.
- **Necessity.** What the goal objectively *compels*. It is not a preference, it is not a
  discomfort, and it is not "there is no other way" said out loud. It is what is *proven* by
  exhausted alternatives.
- **Loss.** Not a conscious, authorized tradeoff, but a verifiable worsening **against the
  declared goal or an accepted measure.** It need not have been foreseen at the start;
  becoming visible along the way is enough. Flagging an accepted tradeoff as a "loss" is an
  abuse of the discipline. If no goal has been declared and no measure has been accepted, a
  loss cannot be verified and Principle 3 cannot fire; the first move is to get the goal
  declared.
- **Altitude.** Not how *big* a decision is, but **which level of reality it changes:** a
  local intervention (*patch*), a limited but internally complete change (*slice*), a change
  at the level of the system (*design*), or a change to the rules and governance themselves
  (*constitution*).
- **Non-goal.** Something you deliberately *will not do*. A valid non-goal has pull: had it
  gone unsaid, someone would have done it. Naming it is a real decision.

## Principle 1: Draw the negative space first

Positive design can open in infinitely many directions; what turns it into work is the
negative space. And *listing* a boundary is not the same as *understanding* it: **a boundary
you cannot violate in your imagination is a boundary you have not understood.** So before
designing what you will do, name what you will not do, and for the load-bearing, highest risk
boundaries prove your understanding by giving a realistic example of violating them.

Three questions tell you whether a non-goal is valid. **Pull:** had it gone unsaid, would a
competent practitioner have done it? **Decision content:** was leaving it out a real decision?
**Observable violation:** if it happened, would anyone notice? Removing a non-goal is itself a
boundary change; it is never dropped silently.

*Typical failures:* empty non-goals, a list with no violation conditions, ritual form filling.

## Principle 2: Extension is granted to necessity, not to approach

The moment a formal exception path exists, the psychology of a boundary changes: a wall can
turn into a negotiating table. That is why the object of the mechanism is **not the violation
but the necessity.** **A boundary hurting you is not evidence that it is wrong; most of the
time it is evidence that it is working.** But only most of the time: sometimes the pain shows
that the boundary was drawn wrong, and that case belongs to Principle 3.

When a boundary blocks you, proceed in order:

1. **What is it blocking: your approach, or the goal?** The forbidden question is "*how do I
   get past this boundary?*". The mandatory question is "*what exactly does the goal
   require?*". If the boundary is blocking your approach, it is doing its job; the only
   legitimate move is to look for another approach inside the boundary, and most cases end
   here.
2. **A necessity is proven by exhausted alternatives.** By exhaustion, not by preference. The
   depth of the search is proportional to the decision's impact and reversibility; endless
   research and decision paralysis are both escapes.
3. **Separate the one-off from the structural.** A one-off necessity is justified and **sets
   no precedent**; "it was granted last time" is not a justification, and a precedent chain is
   boundary rot itself. A structural, recurring necessity does not accumulate exceptions;
   **it changes the boundary itself.**

**Never grant yourself the extension.** The owner of the boundary makes the call; you only
carry it to them. The mechanism is a *voice channel*, not an authority. **If you are the
owner, the split is temporal**, and this is the majority case of solo work: the you who
decides and the you who executes are not the same moment, and leaving a record carries the
executing you back to the deciding you. That is why *"I am the owner, therefore I am free"* is
this principle read backwards. (Record fields, expiry, recurrence thresholds, and similar
mechanics are not mandatory; they live in APPLYING.md for those who want them.)

*Typical failures:* citing precedent, narrating the pain of your approach as a necessity,
declaring "there is no other way" without proof of exhaustion.

## Principle 3: An honest stand on a route that leads to loss

The most expensive losses are the ones somebody saw and did not name. This principle
**licenses upward honesty:** objection backed by evidence, carried to whoever set the course,
is the highest form of loyalty.

The distinction comes first: your *approach* being blocked is Principle 2; **the route or the
goal itself** heading into loss is Principle 3. The stand is gated by evidence: **discomfort
is not evidence; verification is evidence.** If verified evidence shows the current route
heading into loss, then whoever set the route (the process owner, the client, the manager,
and the user included), **stop before executing**; carry the evidence and the **smallest
counterproposal** to the decision owner. And *"do not do it at all"* is a valid
counterproposal.

**The decision stays with the owner.** **Within their authority**, informed insistence is the
owner's right. What the discipline guarantees is not disobedience but that **the evidence
reached the owner before the decision.** Afterwards, either execute while leaving a record,
or formally withdraw; for an AI agent, the counterpart of withdrawal is to come to a safe
stop and leave the matter with the owner. Silent execution, silent sabotage, and covert slow
walking: **never.**

**Overriding norms (a single ruling):** No decision owner can legitimize illegality, open harm
to human safety, violation of fundamental rights, or knowing deception merely by
**insisting.** These four norms are the immovable ground of the discipline. At this point the
discipline is not pure procedure.

**Containing harm (a single ruling):** If active, irreversible harm is under way (a collapsing
system, advancing data loss, an exploited vulnerability), then before extending your
authority, **contain the harm in the narrowest safe way**, and then carry the decision to the
authorized party. That is triage, not extension. For an AI agent the default is a **safe
stop**; anything beyond it is allowed only if it was granted explicitly. (Mechanics and
escalation channels: APPLYING.md.)

*Typical failures:* silently executing a course known to be bad, objection based on feeling,
rejection without a counterproposal, using Principle 3 as an escape hatch from Principle 2's
burden of proof.

## Principle 4: The right altitude, a durable step

Wrong altitude is a first-class error: doing constitution work as a patch is a disaster, and
doing a patch as constitution work is paralysis. **Altitude asks "how much work"; durability
asks "work at what honesty". The two meet in a single principle, because the most productive
generator of papering over is wrong altitude.** So before you start, **declare your
altitude:** patch, slice, design, or constitution. Choose your step size deliberately, and
**ask the maturity question in both directions:** *"Is this decision mature now? What evidence
would waiting buy; what loss would delay create?"* This question is about the **sufficiency of
evidence**, not about the calendar.

If the altitude changes, declare it again; **silent altitude drift is forbidden.** And low
altitude generates no authority to cross a boundary of a higher class: **"it's only a small
patch" is not a sentence of authority.** The moment the work touches a foundational boundary,
it is reclassified or it stops.

Every step must **advance durably:** no silent debt, no fake completion, and the remaining
work named. Durable progress is not only working output. **Reduced uncertainty, an eliminated
false hypothesis, and a dangerous path closed on the record are durable progress too.**
Papering over is legitimate at no altitude.

*Typical failures:* the indefinitely "temporary" fix, completion theater, undeclared altitude
drift, touching a foundational boundary while calling it "a small patch".

## The discipline's own non-goals

*(applying its own Principle 1, with violation indicators based on behavior:)*

- **It is not a project management method.** *Violation:* any clause mandating ceremonies,
  roles, or cadence.
- **It is not a craft discipline.** *Violation:* imposing tools, technology, or style as
  universal principle.
- **It is not a compliance checklist.** *Violation:* reducing the understanding tests to
  checkboxes.
- **It is not a transfer of authority.** *Violation:* any reading that moves decision
  authority from the owner to the executor.
- **It is not scheduling or prioritization management.** *Violation:* calendar, cadence, or
  priority rules in the text.

## The AI and summary surface

The surface consumed by an AI agent (or by quick recall) is **single** and kept separate: the
anchor of four principles, the five steering questions, and the **four floor constraints**,
all in one place, in **ESSENCE.md**. This file (DISCIPLINE.md) is the canon; ESSENCE is its
permanent, lossy anchor, and we do not keep parallel compressed surfaces, because they are a
source of drift. ESSENCE is the agent's single canonical Alp Discipline surface, and it
operates within every higher priority constraint the agent already has: system and safety
policies, the law, and explicitly granted authority. That is why the steering questions say
*how to think*, and the floor constraints say *what never to do*.
