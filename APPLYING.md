# Applying the Discipline — optional mechanics

**Nothing in this file is mandatory.** [DISCIPLINE.md](DISCIPLINE.md) is complete without it.
This file exists for practitioners who *want* concrete mechanics; adopt any entry, adapt it, or
ignore it entirely.

> **Growth rule (v1):** this file holds at most 3–4 entries. A new entry is admitted only for a
> **proven requirement** — a question actually asked in real usage. Mechanics born from imagined
> abuse (fake emergencies, gamed triggers, sabotage semantics) do not enter this file; that work
> belongs to audit and security, not to the discipline.

## 1. Necessity records

When a necessity opens a boundary (Principle 2), leave a record. Useful fields:

- **Boundary** — which line was opened.
- **Declared goal** — what the goal objectively compelled.
- **Alternatives exhausted** — each in-boundary alternative tried or considered, and why it
  fails *against the goal* (not against your comfort).
- **One-off or structural** — a one-off sets no precedent; a structural necessity goes to the
  owner as a proposed boundary change.
- **Expiry / review date** — when this record stops being valid on its own.

**Recurrence threshold:** when the "same one-off" appears for the third time, treat it as
structural — stop recording exceptions and take a boundary change to its owner. In solo work,
the record is the voice channel between the deciding-you and the executing-you.

## 2. Containment and escalation

Principle 3's containment ruling, in practice. Decide these *in advance*, per context:

- **Who owns each hard and foundational boundary** — an escalation with no addressee is a
  shout into the void.
- **What travels with an escalation** — the verification (not the feeling), the impact, and
  the smallest counter-proposal.
- **What happens when the owner is unreachable** — the default remains safe-stop; containment
  acts only within existing authority, in the narrowest safe form, and the decision still
  travels to the owner afterwards. For an AI agent, anything beyond safe-stop must be
  explicitly granted in advance — never inferred in the moment.

## 3. Proportional exhaustion

How deep must the alternative search go before a necessity counts as proven? **Proportional to
impact × reversibility.**

- Reversible and low-impact: minutes of thought and one honest sentence are enough.
- Irreversible or high-impact: a written search — what was tried or considered, and why each
  alternative fails against the goal.

Both endless research and an instant "there is no other way" are escapes; the proportionality
rule is what makes exhaustion a practice rather than a slogan.
